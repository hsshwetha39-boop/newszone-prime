Chiranjeevi Meets Amit Shah Over AI Deepfake Misuse, Seeks Stronger Protection for Artists

By Shwetha • November 4, 2026 • 4 min read

Megastar Chiranjeevi has taken his concerns over the growing misuse of artificial intelligence and deepfake technology directly to the Central Government. The Telugu film star, accompanied by his son and actor Ram Charan, met Union Home Minister Amit Shah in Hyderabad on November 2 and discussed the increasing threat posed by digitally manipulated videos and AI-generated content targeting artists and the entertainment industry.

During the meeting, Chiranjeevi highlighted how artificial intelligence tools are increasingly being misused to create manipulated images, videos and other content without the consent of the individuals involved. He expressed concern that such practices could seriously damage an artist's reputation, personal dignity, professional identity and creative rights.

Union Minister G. Kishan Reddy was also present during the meeting. Chiranjeevi and Ram Charan reportedly requested stronger legal safeguards and strict action against individuals who misuse AI technology to create or circulate objectionable and misleading content targeting artists.

Following the meeting, Chiranjeevi shared details of the discussion through a social media post. He expressed gratitude to Amit Shah for listening to their concerns and said the Home Minister had assured them that the government would examine the issue urgently and work towards introducing stronger protections and strict penalties against those responsible for such misuse.

Chiranjeevi also revealed that Amit Shah had advised them to submit a formal written representation explaining the concerns in detail. The Home Minister entrusted G. Kishan Reddy with coordinating the matter so that the issue could be examined further.

The actor described the response as an encouraging development for the film fraternity and expressed hope that the discussion would eventually lead to meaningful protection for artists and their creative work.

Ram Charan also shared photographs from the meeting and spoke about the importance of addressing the growing misuse of AI and deepfake technology. He said he was encouraged by the Home Minister's understanding of the concerns raised by the father-son duo and appreciated the assurance that the government would examine the matter urgently.

Ram Charan emphasised that digitally manipulated content could have a serious impact not only on individual actors but also on the wider entertainment industry. He welcomed the government's willingness to consider stronger safeguards and strict action against those responsible for abusing these technologies.

The meeting comes amid increasing concerns within the Indian entertainment industry over the misuse of artificial intelligence. Deepfake technology can digitally recreate a person's face, voice or appearance and place them in situations that never actually occurred. Although these tools have legitimate applications in filmmaking and creative production, their misuse can result in misleading, humiliating or damaging material being circulated online.

Actors and other public figures are particularly vulnerable because their photographs, videos and voices are widely available online. Manipulated content can spread rapidly through social media platforms, making it difficult for victims to control its circulation or prevent further misuse.

One widely discussed example involved actress Rashmika Mandanna, whose face was digitally inserted into a video that circulated online in 2023. The incident attracted widespread attention and renewed discussions about the dangers of deepfake technology. In January 2024, Delhi Police arrested Eemani Naveen, a 24-year-old engineering graduate from Andhra Pradesh, in connection with the creation and circulation of the manipulated video.

The incident highlighted how easily artificial intelligence can be used to create misleading material involving real individuals. It also brought attention to the difficulties faced by victims who must deal with reputational damage, privacy concerns and the rapid spread of manipulated content.

Chiranjeevi's latest meeting with Amit Shah has brought these concerns back into focus, particularly the need to protect the identity and dignity of people working in the entertainment industry. The issue extends beyond film actors and can affect singers, public personalities, content creators and other individuals whose images and voices can be digitally manipulated.

The demand for stronger safeguards also raises questions about accountability. Artists and industry representatives are increasingly concerned about how victims can report deepfake material, how quickly harmful content can be removed and what penalties should apply to people who deliberately create or distribute such material without permission.

The government has assured Chiranjeevi and Ram Charan that their concerns will be examined urgently. However, the meeting does not yet represent the announcement of a new law or a confirmed set of penalties. A formal written representation is expected to help communicate the concerns in greater detail before further steps are considered.

For the film fraternity, the discussion represents an important effort to bring the issue of AI misuse and deepfakes to the attention of policymakers. As artificial intelligence technology continues to develop, protecting personal identity, creative ownership and individual dignity is becoming an increasingly important challenge.

Chiranjeevi's meeting with Amit Shah has therefore placed the protection of artists against AI-generated manipulation firmly in the spotlight. The next steps will depend on the government's examination of the concerns and whether stronger safeguards and penalties can be introduced to address the growing threat of deepfake misuse in India.

Sources: statements shared on social media by Chiranjeevi and Ram Charan, and reports by IANS, Outlook India, Gulf News and Free Press Journal.
